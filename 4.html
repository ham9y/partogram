WHO Partogram Guide

By Dr. Hamdy

A practical guide for the labour record

Complete the partogram from the clinical record as labour progresses. Plot observations at the time they are taken, keep entries in chronological order, and record the actual value rather than a guess.

Clinical safety: This is an educational documentation guide. Use the current WHO form, local policy, senior review, and emergency pathways for clinical decisions. A partogram supports assessment; it does not replace bedside assessment.

Step-by-step: completing the partogram

1

Prepare the form

Use the current approved WHO or local partogram. Confirm the patient identity and make sure the date, time, units, and chart legend are visible before writing.

2

Complete the patient header

Write the name, age, gravida, para, and hospital number from the clinical record. Check these identifiers against the patient before continuing.

3

Set the time reference

Enter the admission date and time. Treat this as the chart's time zero. Record the ROM time separately if membranes have ruptured, and note whether the event was spontaneous or artificial when required.

4

Record baseline examination

Enter the admission cervical dilatation in cm and descent in fifths. Use the documented examination result and record the examiner or examination time according to local policy.

5

Record treatment already started

Write the IV fluid and rate. If oxytocin is running, record its concentration and infusion rate exactly as prescribed. Add analgesia or other interventions in the notes or designated treatment area.

6

Start one observation row

At the time of each assessment, find the matching time on the horizontal axis and use one row or vertical time position for that assessment. Do not move a value to make it fit a preferred pattern.

7

Plot fetal heart rate

Enter the measured FHR in bpm in the fetal-heart-rate section at the correct time. Use the form's marker and connect sequential points only when the form instructs you to do so.

8

Plot cervical dilatation and descent

Plot cervical dilatation with the form's X marker and descent with the form's O marker. Connect each series in time order. Keep the two scales separate: cervical dilatation is in cm; descent is in fifths.

9

Fill liquor and moulding

Use the form legend to enter the liquor symbol and the moulding grade found on examination. Record a blank only when no observation was made; do not use a blank to mean normal.

10

Count contractions

Count the number of contractions in a 10-minute window. Fill the half-hour column that contains the observation and use the form's shading or pattern to show contraction duration.

11

Plot maternal observations

Plot pulse on the maternal pulse scale. Plot both systolic and diastolic BP as shown on the form. Add the measured temperature at the required hourly position, using the actual measurement time.

12

Document changes and decisions

Record membrane rupture, examinations, medication or oxytocin changes, analgesia, fluid changes, reviews, transfer, operative decisions, delivery, or CS time in the appropriate area and clinical notes.

13

Review before signing

Check that every entry has the correct time and unit, trends are connected in order, abnormal findings are acknowledged, missing critical observations are followed up, and the chart is signed according to local policy.

Recommended workflow

1. IdentifyWrite patient, admission, parity, and hospital details.
2. Set the clockUse one admission date and time consistently for every row.
3. ObserveRecord fetal, labour, and maternal observations at the bedside.
4. ReviewConnect trends, check the whole patient, and escalate concerns.

Patient and admission fields

IDENTITY

Name, age, gravida, para

Copy the patient identifiers from the chart or wristband. Gravida is the number of pregnancies; para records previous births according to local documentation convention.

REFERENCE

Hospital number

Use the official hospital or medical record number. Check it against the patient record before plotting.

START TIME

Admission date and time

Enter the agreed reference time used by the team. All plotted hours must be calculated from this same starting point.

MEMBRANES

ROM time

Record the time membranes ruptured when known. Document whether the time is spontaneous, artificial, or uncertain according to local practice.

BASELINE

Cervix at admission

Enter the first documented cervical dilatation in centimetres. Use the examination finding, not an estimate based on labour duration.

BASELINE

Descent at admission

Record fetal head descent in fifths palpable above the pelvic brim. Keep the scale convention consistent throughout the chart.

TREATMENT

Fluids and oxytocin

Record fluid type and rate. If oxytocin is used, include concentration and drops or milliunits per minute as required by the local form.

OUTCOME

Delivery time or time of CS

Enter the actual time of birth or caesarean section. Observations after that event should not be plotted as intrapartum observations.

How to fill the plotted areas

FETAL HEART RATE

FHR, in beats per minute

Plot each auscultated or monitored FHR value against the correct time. Use the trend with the clinical assessment; investigate persistent abnormalities according to local protocol.

CERVIX

Cervical dilatation, X

Plot the examination result in centimetres at the examination time and connect sequential points. The X marks cervical change; do not move a point to make the line look smoother.

DESCENT

Descent, O

Plot descent in fifths using the form's scale and use O markers. Interpret it alongside position, station, moulding, contractions, and the examination findings.

LIQUOR

Liquor

Enter the locally approved symbols for clear, meconium-stained, blood-stained, or absent liquor. Use the legend on the form and document uncertainty in the notes.

MOULDING

Moulding

Record the degree found on vaginal examination using the form's symbols, such as 0, +, ++, or +++. Increasing moulding needs clinical assessment in context.

CONTRACTIONS

Contractions per 10 minutes

Count contractions in a 10-minute window and fill the matching half-hour cell. Use shading or patterns to show duration according to the form legend.

PULSE

Maternal pulse

Plot pulse at the time measured on the maternal vitals section. Look for a trend and compare it with pain, temperature, bleeding, hydration, and blood pressure.

BLOOD PRESSURE

BP, systolic/diastolic

Plot both systolic and diastolic values at the observation time, connected as shown on the form. Record the actual cuff reading and repeat or escalate according to local protocol.

TEMPERATURE

Temperature

Plot the measured temperature at the correct hourly position when the form requires hourly plotting. Note the route and timing in the clinical record if relevant.

Useful completion hints

Use actual times

Do not backdate a point to the nearest grid line. The time of the examination or measurement is the time to plot.

Make units visible

Check cm, bpm, minutes, degrees, mmHg, and the contraction counting window before accepting an entry.

Keep the trend honest

Connect observations in order, but never redraw a point because the pattern looks unusual.

Annotate events

Mark examinations, membrane rupture, analgesia, oxytocin changes, transfer, review, and operative decisions in the notes.

Check missing data

A blank cell means no documented observation, not a normal result. Follow up missing critical observations.

Review the whole picture

Compare fetal status, cervical change, contractions, maternal observations, and treatment rather than relying on one line.

Benefits of a well-completed partogram

Shared situational awareness

The team can see labour progress and recent observations in one place.

Earlier recognition of change

Trends may make deterioration, poor progress, or treatment effects easier to notice.

Clear handover

A chronological record supports concise communication during shift changes or transfer.

Better documentation

Time-stamped observations and interventions create a clearer clinical record.

When to pause and seek review

Seek timely senior or emergency review when there is fetal compromise, maternal instability, significant bleeding, severe hypertension, fever or suspected infection, obstructed labour, concerning moulding, abnormal labour progress, or any change that does not fit the clinical picture. Follow local escalation pathways.

Educational reference only. Always use the current approved WHO or local partogram and clinical protocol.
